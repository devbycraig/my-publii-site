Why You Should Print Your Pictures

Maria Vernon

Today’s children are the most photographed generation in history.

Yet paradoxically, they have little or no real access to those photos.

Today, children are growing up in a society of picture takers who are not picture printers. While snapping a picture has never been easier or cheaper, and printing is fast and inexpensive, the reality is: we simply don't print.

This modern phenomenon is a quiet tragedy for young children who rely on tangible connections to feel secure and loved.

1. Why Printed Pictures Truly Matter for Preschoolers

In days gone by pictures were special. Taking a picture was an event and the prints were valued and hung on walls or collected in albums.

Pictures were shared … and this practice is vital for a child's sense of self and belonging. Photographs show a family as an extended unit and help young children see themselves as an important and valued part. Seeing these physical images helps them feel a crucial sense of security and belonging.

There is a well-known study called Phototherapy in Mental Health by David A. Krauss and Jerry L. Fryrear.  

Within the pages of it, David Krauss, a licensed psychologist, says: 

“I think it is really important to show a family as a family unit. It is so helpful for children to see themselves as a valued and important part of that family unit.  …  Kids get it on a really simple level.”  

And speaking for myself, when I worked with the babies as an early childhood teacher I would often ask our parents to bring in photos of themselves - particularly when their child was not settling. I would laminate the photos and display them where their child was able to access it. It always amazed me how many times a child would carry their photo of mum and/or dad. The child did not have their parents physically with them but the photo was a real comfort.

Of course I would sometimes find the photo discarded on the floor, and the photos became well worn and dog-eared, but they worked!

2. The “Problem” with Phones

The vast collection of photos we take is mostly locked up in smartphones, making the photos inaccessible to young children. To even view them, a young child would need:

  • To know they are there.
  • To have permission.
  • To have the password.
  • To sort through large numbers of photos (that are not relevant).
  • To hold (and play with!) an expensive phone.

The entire process is dependent on adult oversight and approval.

A physical photo, however, can belong to the child. They can connect with family members when they want to, or when they need a little time with someone special.

3. Give Them Their Own Photos

It’s important that the photos belong to your child. The photos are not meant to be guarded or pampered—they can easily be replaced. They should be located where the child can find them, and if your child thrashes the photos, well done. Mission accomplished!

What kind of photos should you print?

  • Photos of close family members: parents, siblings, uncles and aunties, cousins, and pets. Don't forget grandparents—anyone your child relates to as family.
  • Photos of things you do together with the child.
  • Photos of things important to your child, like favorite toys at home, games you play, and places you go together.
  • Photos of things your child connects with you (like mom’s sewing machine or dad’s tools)—but make sure the people are in them.

The photos don’t need to be studio quality shots. Your child won’t care about your hair, clothes, or smile; the fact that the photos end up “broken and bent” is not disrespectful. But if you want to get more life out of the photos, purchasing a small home laminator is a really good idea.

4. Creative Ideas for Printed Photos

Getting photos in the hands of your child can be fun and creative. Here are a few easy, accessible ideas:

  • Laminated Photos with Magnets : Print photos, place self-adhesive magnets on the back, laminate (to prevent little fingers from pulling off the magnets), and trim, remembering to round the corners.
  • Portable Photo Cards : Glue small photos onto colored card (both sides), laminate, punch a hole, and use a split ring or string to hold them together.
  • Photos in Containers : Print and laminate photos, then place them in child-size boxes or plastic containers (containers with handles allow children to carry you with them!).
  • Matching Photo Cards : Print two copies of each photo, glue them onto cardboard backing, laminate, and cut into squares to be used for games like “Memory Pairs” or “Snap”.

Summary: You Are Part of Their World

There are many advantages to putting family photos in the hands of your preschooler. Your children can connect with their wider family whenever they feel the need and feel part of a family that protects, cares for, and values them.
